> **Purpose:** demonstrate an end-to-end population-health analytics workflow — from raw, messy
> patient-level extracts to a governed star schema, a DAX measure library and a dashboard design
> that helps a care-management team move from *describing* a population to *acting* on it.

## 1. Why population health, why this dashboard

Population health management turns a health system's attention from the patients who *show up* to
the whole population it is *accountable for*. The operating questions are always the same:

1. **Who is in our population and what is their disease burden?** — prevalence of diabetes,
   hypertension and obesity, multimorbidity, and how well each condition is controlled.
2. **Where are the preventive-care gaps?** — guideline-recommended screenings (USPSTF / HEDIS) that
   eligible patients have not completed, and the disparities behind those gaps.
3. **Which levers are working?** — digital tools (patient app, reminders, wearables) are only worth
   funding if they change screening and self-management behaviour.
4. **Who do we call first?** — a transparent risk stratification that ranks outreach by expected
   benefit, not by who happened to book an appointment.

This project answers those four questions with a reproducible pipeline and a dashboard design that a
Power BI developer can build directly from the spec.

## 2. Dataset

The data are **fully synthetic**, generated by `src/generate_synthetic_data.py` using a
Synthea-style schema (SNOMED-CT condition codes, patient-level demographics and geography) with
epidemiologically plausible relationships: prevalence rises with age, BMI and area deprivation;
screening uptake falls with deprivation and lack of a primary-care provider and rises with digital
enrolment; wearable step counts fall with age, BMI and chronic burden and follow a seasonal curve.

| Source table | Grain | Rows (raw → clean) | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| `patients` | one row per patient | 50,320 → 50,000 | age, sex, race/ethnicity, region, state, urbanicity, ADI decile, insurance, smoking, height/weight/BMI, PCP, app & wearable flags |
| `conditions` | one row per patient-condition | 53,669 → 52,746 | diabetes, hypertension, obesity, hyperlipidemia, asthma with latest clinical measure (A1c, BP, LDL, BMI) and medication flag |
| `screenings` | one row per eligible patient-screening | 294,717 | 10 preventive services with eligibility, completion, last/next date, reminder flag |
| `wearable_monthly` | one row per patient-month with a synced device | 308,034 → 307,534 | average daily steps, sleep hours, resting heart rate, active days, 10k-step days, device |
| `engagement_events` | one row per app event | 297,776 → 295,436 | logins, appointment bookings, medication reminders sent/acknowledged, screening-reminder views, care-team messages, wearable syncs |

The generator deliberately injects the defects analysts meet in real extracts — duplicate rows,
inconsistent coding (`SOUTH`, ` south `, `female`), states as full names, missing and impossible
BMI values, future birth dates, negative step counts, orphan and out-of-window events. The
before/after counts are in [`docs/data_quality_scorecard.csv`](docs/data_quality_scorecard.csv).

Regenerate with a different size or seed:

```bash
python src/generate_synthetic_data.py --n-patients 50000 --seed 42 --out data/raw
```

## 3. Pipeline: from raw extract to star schema

`notebooks/01_clean_and_build_star_schema.ipynb` is the single source of truth for every cleaning
rule and derived field. Highlights:

* **Deterministic cleaning** — canonical labels, plausibility ranges per metric (steps 1–50,000;
  sleep 2–16 h; resting HR 35–200 bpm; BMI 12–70), date sanity vs the 31 Dec 2025 snapshot.
* **Conservative clinical logic** — a screening only counts if documented with a valid date inside
  the guideline interval; obesity is re-derived from cleaned BMI so the two views agree; control
  status uses BP < 140/90, HbA1c < 8.0 %, LDL < 130 mg/dL.
* **Explainable risk stratification** — an additive points model over age, chronic burden, obesity
  class, disease control, smoking, inactivity, open care gaps, deprivation and PCP access, bucketed
  into Low / Moderate / High / Very High. Each component is stored on
  `fact_patient_risk_snapshot` so the dashboard can show *why* a patient is high risk.
* **Star schema with integrity checks** — integer surrogate keys, a 1980–2036 calendar, and
  assertions that every foreign key resolves before export.

```
                     dim_date
                        │
dim_geography ── dim_patient ──┬── fact_screening ─────── dim_screening
                               ├── fact_condition ─────── dim_condition
                               ├── fact_wearable_monthly
                               ├── fact_engagement_event ─ dim_event_type
                               └── fact_patient_risk_snapshot
```

Column-level documentation is auto-generated in [`docs/data_dictionary.md`](docs/data_dictionary.md).

### Run it

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python src/generate_synthetic_data.py                       # ~15 s → data/raw/
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace \
    notebooks/01_clean_and_build_star_schema.ipynb          # ~30 s → data/star_schema/, docs/
```

## 4. Dashboard design

Full detail in [`powerbi/dashboard_spec.md`](powerbi/dashboard_spec.md). Eight pages share a synced
slicer rail (region, state, urbanicity, age band, sex, chronic condition, risk tier, insurance,
deprivation band, engagement segment, reporting month):

| # | Page | Core question | Headline KPIs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Executive Overview | How healthy is the population and are the levers working? | Total patients · Any focus condition % · Screening rate · App engagement rate · High-risk share · Open care gaps |
| 2 | Chronic Disease Prevalence & Control | Where is the burden and how well is it controlled? | Prevalence by condition, age, state, deprivation · Control rate |
| 3 | Preventive Screening & Care Gaps | Which screenings are we missing, for whom? | Screening rate vs target · Care gaps · Days overdue · Digital engagement lift |
| 4 | Digital Engagement | Who uses the app and does it drive care actions? | Enrollment · Engagement rate · MAU · Bookings · Reminder ack rate · Dormancy |
| 5 | Wearable Vitals & Lifestyle | What do devices reveal about activity, sleep and HR? | Adoption · Steps · Sleep · Resting HR · 10k-step months |
| 6 | Risk Stratification & Outreach | Who should the care team call this week? | Tier distribution · High-risk share · Rising risk · Priority outreach cohort |
| 7 | Data Quality & Lineage | Can we trust the numbers? | Before/after scorecard, imputation rates |
| 8 | Patient list (drill-through) | Which individual patients sit behind a bar? | Ranked, exportable worklist |

The measure library in `powerbi/measures.dax` is organised in the same groups (population,
prevalence & control, screening, engagement, wearables, risk, data quality, dynamic titles) with a
recommended format string on each measure.

## 5. What the synthetic population shows (snapshot 31 Dec 2025)

Numbers come from `docs/summary_stats.json`, written by the notebook.

**Population** — 50,000 adults, median age 46, 51.6 % female, 13.4 % aged 65+; South 38 %, West 24 %,
Midwest 21 %, Northeast 17 %.

**Chronic disease burden**

| Condition | Prevalence | Controlled |
|---|---|---|
| Obesity (BMI ≥ 30) | 34.9 % | — |
| Hypertension | 25.9 % | 51.2 % (BP < 140/90) |
| Diabetes | 12.1 % | 63.2 % (A1c < 8 %) |
| Hyperlipidemia | 25.9 % | 37.9 % (LDL < 130) |
| Any of the three focus conditions | 50.1 % | |
| Two or more chronic conditions | 18.3 % | |

The South carries the heaviest burden on every measure (29.3 % hypertension, 14.5 % diabetes, 39.8 %
obesity vs 23–25 %, 10–11 % and 29–34 % elsewhere), and hypertension prevalence climbs from 6 % of
18–29-year-olds to 78 % of those 80+.

**Preventive care** — overall screening completion is 65.2 % against a 75 % target, leaving
102,654 open care gaps and 87 % of patients with at least one. Blood-pressure screening (81.6 %) and
mammography (72.7 %) perform best; flu vaccination (51.8 %) and diabetic eye exams (54.4 %) worst.
The Northeast leads (71.9 %) and the South trails (61.0 %).

**Digital engagement as a lever** — 66.5 % of patients are enrolled in the app and 56.1 % of those are
active in three or more months. Enrolled patients complete screenings at 69.6 % versus 56.9 % for
the unenrolled, a **12.7-point lift**. Monthly active users grew from 7,083 in January to 16,452 in
December; 70.6 % of medication reminders were acknowledged and 26,515 appointments were booked
in-app.

**Wearables** — 55.6 % of patients sync a device. Median daily steps are 7,711 with a clear seasonal
swing (6,800 in January → 8,400 in June); only 14 % of device-months average 10,000+ steps. Steps
fall monotonically with risk tier: 8,493 (Low) → 7,703 → 6,720 → 5,372 (Very High).

**Risk stratification** — 32.6 % Low, 37.2 % Moderate, 18.4 % High, 11.7 % Very High. The higher the
tier, the lower the screening rate (78 % → 57 %) and the lower the engagement (46 % → 24 %) — the
patients who would benefit most from preventive care are the least reached by the digital channel,
which is exactly the cohort the outreach list on page 6 surfaces.

## 6. Analytical caveats

* Synthetic data: relationships are plausible but calibrated by hand, not estimated from real
  claims or EHR data. Use the numbers to validate the pipeline and dashboard, not to draw clinical
  conclusions.
* Screening eligibility uses simplified age/sex rules; real HEDIS specifications add exclusions
  (hospice, bilateral mastectomy, frailty) and continuous-enrolment requirements.
* The risk score is a transparent heuristic, deliberately not a predictive model. In production it
  would sit alongside a calibrated model (e.g. logistic or gradient-boosted risk of avoidable
  admission) and be validated for fairness across region, race/ethnicity and deprivation.
* Wearable data covers device users only; page 5 always states the denominator to avoid selection
  bias being read as a population result.
